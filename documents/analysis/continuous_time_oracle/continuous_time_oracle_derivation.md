# Continuous-time delayed-LQR and Pontryagin oracles for value-gradient control of delay systems

**Scope.** This note derives, with explicit hypotheses and proofs, the reference oracles used to
test the value-gradient (Doya) control law on delay systems: (i) the *linear* continuous-time
delayed-LQR oracle (platoon; Mackey–Glass linearised), and (ii) the *nonlinear* continuous-time
optimal-control oracle (Mackey–Glass) obtained from Pontryagin's principle. The central object is the
optimal value functional $V^\star$ and its gradient; the central requirement is that Doya's law
$u=\tfrac12 R^{-1}B^\top\partial_xV^\star$ reproduce the optimal control **exactly**, with no
time-discretisation artefact. We make that requirement precise (Proposition 4 and its Corollary) and
quantify the artefact it removes (Remark 4.1).

Throughout, $|\cdot|$ is the Euclidean norm, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ the Euclidean inner product,
$\preceq$ the Loewner order, and for a $C^1$ scalar field $\Phi$, $\partial_x\Phi$ is the gradient
(column vector). Matrices: $A_0,A_1\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$, $B\in\mathbb R^{d\times m}$,
$Q\succeq0$, $R\succ0$.

---

## 1. The controlled delay system and the optimal control problem

Fix a delay $\tau>0$. Consider the controlled retarded functional differential equation (RFDE)
$$
\dot x(t)=\mathcal F\big(x(t),x(t-\tau)\big)+Bu(t),\qquad t\ge0,\quad x(t)\in\mathbb R^d,\ u(t)\in\mathbb R^m,
\tag{1}
$$
with initial datum the history segment $x_0=\phi\in C([-\tau,0];\mathbb R^d)$. We treat two cases:

* **Linear (L):** $\mathcal F(x,y)=A_0x+A_1y$. (Platoon: $d=2N$; the linearised forms $A_0,A_1$ are
  given by the plant. Mackey–Glass about its equilibrium: see §6.)
* **Nonlinear (N):** $\mathcal F$ is $C^2$ with an equilibrium $x^\star$ and control $u^\star_{\mathrm{eq}}$
  such that $\mathcal F(x^\star,x^\star)+Bu^\star_{\mathrm{eq}}=0$. (Mackey–Glass; see §6.)

Given $Q\succeq0$, $R\succ0$, $x_{\mathrm{tg}}\in\mathbb R^d$, the objective is the infinite-horizon cost
$$
J(\phi;u)=\int_0^\infty\Big[(x(t)-x_{\mathrm{tg}})^\top Q\,(x(t)-x_{\mathrm{tg}})+u(t)^\top R\,u(t)\Big]\,dt .
\tag{2}
$$
The **value** is $J^\star(\phi)=\inf_u J(\phi;u)$. We use the *reward* convention $V^\star:=-J^\star$
when stating Doya's law, and the *cost-to-go* convention $V^\star_{\mathrm c}:=J^\star$ when stating
the Riccati/Pontryagin equations; the two differ only by sign.

> **Remark 1.1 (why time must stay continuous).** Doya's law is a statement about the *continuous-time*
> Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) stationarity. Any reference value obtained from a *discretised-in-time*
> Bellman equation introduces an $O(\Delta t)$ rescaling of the gradient that corrupts the control
> magnitude (Remark 4.1). The constructions below therefore discretise only the *history variable*
> $\theta$, never time.

---

## 2. Reformulation as an abstract Cauchy problem

The state of (1) at time $t$ is the history segment $x_t:\theta\mapsto x(t+\theta)$, $\theta\in[-\tau,0]$.
On the Banach space $\mathcal X:=C([-\tau,0];\mathbb R^d)$ the **uncontrolled linear** flow is the
$C_0$-semigroup generated by
$$
\mathcal A\phi=\phi',\qquad
D(\mathcal A)=\big\{\phi\in C^1([-\tau,0];\mathbb R^d):\ \phi'(0)=A_0\phi(0)+A_1\phi(-\tau)\big\},
\tag{3}
$$
the boundary condition at $\theta=0$ encoding the dynamics; on the interior $\mathcal A$ is pure
transport (shift). This is the classical reformulation of a linear RFDE (Hale–Verduyn Lunel,
*Introduction to Functional Differential Equations*, Ch. 7). For the LQR theory one works instead on
the Hilbert space $\mathcal M^2:=\mathbb R^d\times L^2([-\tau,0];\mathbb R^d)$ (the structural state
space of Delfour–Mitter / Bensoussan–Da Prato–Delfour–Mitter), on which the controlled linear system
is an abstract LTI $\dot{\mathsf z}=\mathsf A\mathsf z+\mathsf B u$ with $\mathsf A$ the $\mathcal M^2$
realisation of (3) and $\mathsf B u=(Bu,0)$; the infinite-dimensional algebraic Riccati operator
equation then has a unique nonnegative stabilising solution under stabilisability/detectability
(Pritchard–Salamon; Curtain–Zwart, *An Introduction to Infinite-Dimensional Linear Systems Theory*,
Ch. 6). We do not solve the operator equation; we approximate $\mathsf A$ by collocation (§3) and solve
the resulting finite CARE, which is the standard, convergent route.

The **nonlinear** flow (case N) is the local semiflow generated by the RFDE; its linearisation about
$(x^\star,x^\star)$ is of the form (3) with $A_0=\partial_x\mathcal F(x^\star,x^\star)$,
$A_1=\partial_y\mathcal F(x^\star,x^\star)$.

## 3. Pseudospectral collocation of the history

We discretise $\theta\in[-\tau,0]$ by Chebyshev collocation.

**Definition 3.1 (nodes and differentiation matrix).** Let $\xi_j=\cos(\pi j/N)$, $j=0,\dots,N$, be the
Chebyshev–Gauss–Lobatto points on $[-1,1]$, and $D_{\mathrm{ch}}\in\mathbb R^{(N+1)\times(N+1)}$ the
associated differentiation matrix (Trefethen, *Spectral Methods in MATLAB*, Ch. 6), exact on
polynomials of degree $\le N$: $(D_{\mathrm{ch}}\,p|_{\xi})_j=p'(\xi_j)$. Map to the history grid by the
affine bijection $\theta_j=\tfrac\tau2(\xi_j-1)\in[-\tau,0]$, so $\theta_0=0$ and $\theta_N=-\tau$. By
the chain rule $\frac{d}{d\theta}=\frac{2}{\tau}\frac{d}{d\xi}$, hence the **history-differentiation
matrix** is
$$
D:=\frac{2}{\tau}\,D_{\mathrm{ch}} .
\tag{4}
$$

> **Caution 3.2 (the $2/\tau$ factor).** Omitting the factor $2/\tau$ in (4) collocates the operator on
> $[-1,1]$ rather than $[-\tau,0]$, i.e. it silently replaces $\tau$ by $2$. For Mackey–Glass this moves
> the system across its Hopf bifurcation ($\tau_c\approx4.71$, §6) and destroys the limit cycle. The
> error is invisible to the internal consistency checks (Proposition 5.3, the costate gate) and is
> detected only by comparison against the true plant (§7, free-response check).

Collocate the history values $z=(\zeta_0,\dots,\zeta_N)\in(\mathbb R^d)^{N+1}\cong\mathbb R^{d(N+1)}$,
$\zeta_j\approx\phi(\theta_j)$, with $\zeta_0=x(t)$ (current state) and $\zeta_N=x(t-\tau)$ (delayed
state). Replacing $\phi'$ by $Dz$ in the interior and the boundary row by the dynamics yields the finite
vector field $F:\mathbb R^{d(N+1)}\times\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^{d(N+1)}$,
$$
F(z,u)_i=(Dz)_i\ \ (i=1,\dots,N),\qquad
F(z,u)_0=\mathcal F(\zeta_0,\zeta_N)+Bu ,
\tag{5}
$$
($D$ acting blockwise via $D\otimes I_d$). The reduced control system is the ODE
$$
\dot z=F(z,u)\quad\text{on }\mathbb R^{d(N+1)} .
\tag{6}
$$
The running cost (2) becomes $\ell(z,u)=(z-z_{\mathrm{tg}})^\top\widehat Q\,(z-z_{\mathrm{tg}})+u^\top Ru$
with $\widehat Q=E_0^\top Q E_0$, $E_0z=\zeta_0$ the current-state projection, and
$z_{\mathrm{tg}}=\mathbf 1\otimes x_{\mathrm{tg}}$.

**Proposition 3.3 (spectral consistency).** *For $\phi\in C^\infty$, the collocation derivative
$Dz$ converges to $\phi'|_{\theta_j}$ faster than any power of $1/N$. Consequently the rightmost
eigenvalues of the linearised collocation generator $M$ (Def. 4.1) converge to the rightmost roots of
the characteristic equation $\det\!\big(sI-A_0-A_1e^{-s\tau}\big)=0$ of the linear RFDE.*

*Justification.* Spectral accuracy of Chebyshev differentiation for smooth functions is classical
(Trefethen, Thm. in Ch. 4–6). Convergence of the collocated spectrum to the RFDE characteristic roots
is the pseudospectral-discretisation-of-the-generator result of Breda–Maset–Vermiglio
(*Stability of Linear Delay Differential Equations: A Numerical Approach with MATLAB*, 2015). $\square$

> **Numerical corroboration.** For Mackey–Glass linearised about $x^\star=1$ ($A_0=-0.1$, $A_1=-0.4$,
> $\tau=6$) the rightmost characteristic root pair is $0.0242\pm0.323\,i$; the collocation $M$ reproduces
> it to four digits already at $N=10$, and is invariant in $N\in\{10,18,30,40\}$.

## 4. The linear–quadratic oracle

Assume case **(L)** (or the linearisation of (N)).

**Definition 4.1 (collocation generator).** $M:=\partial_zF(\cdot)\in\mathbb R^{d(N+1)\times d(N+1)}$ is
$D\otimes I_d$ with block-row $0$ replaced by $[\,A_0,\ 0,\dots,0,\ A_1\,]$; the input matrix is
$\mathcal N:=E_0^\top B$ (control enters only the current-state block). The reduced LTI is
$\dot{\delta z}=M\,\delta z+\mathcal N u$, $\delta z:=z-z^\star$.

**Hypothesis 4.2.** $(M,\mathcal N)$ is stabilisable and $(\widehat Q^{1/2},M)$ is detectable.

> These hold in our cells: $\mathcal N=E_0^\top B$ actuates the current-state block and the transport
> propagates it through the history (reachability of the augmented chain); $\widehat Q$ penalises the
> current state, observable through the same chain. They are the finite-dimensional shadows of the
> infinite-dimensional stabilisability/detectability of §2.

**Proposition 4.3 (LQR value).** *Under Hypothesis 4.2 the continuous algebraic Riccati equation*
$$
M^\top P_c+P_cM-P_c\,\mathcal N R^{-1}\mathcal N^\top P_c+\widehat Q=0
\tag{7}
$$
*has a unique solution $P_c\succeq0$ with $M-\mathcal NR^{-1}\mathcal N^\top P_c$ Hurwitz. The optimal
cost-to-go of $\dot{\delta z}=M\delta z+\mathcal Nu$ with cost
$\int_0^\infty(\delta z^\top\widehat Q\delta z+u^\top Ru)\,dt$ is*
$$
V^\star_{\mathrm c}(\delta z)=\delta z^\top P_c\,\delta z,
\qquad
u^\star=-K_c\,\delta z,\quad K_c=R^{-1}\mathcal N^\top P_c .
\tag{8}
$$

*Proof.* Classical finite-dimensional LQR (Anderson–Moore, *Optimal Control: Linear Quadratic Methods*,
Ch. 3); existence/uniqueness of the stabilising $P_c$ under Hypothesis 4.2 is the standard CARE theorem
(Lancaster–Rodman, *Algebraic Riccati Equations*, Thm. 9.1.2). $\square$

**Proposition 4.4 (independent verification of $P_c$).** *Let $A_{\mathrm{cl}}=M-\mathcal NK_c$ and
$S=\widehat Q+K_c^\top RK_c$. Then $P_c$ is the unique solution of the Lyapunov equation*
$$
A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top P+PA_{\mathrm{cl}}+S=0,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\delta z_0^\top P_c\,\delta z_0=\int_0^\infty \delta z(t)^\top S\,\delta z(t)\,dt
\tag{9}
$$
*along $\delta z(t)=e^{A_{\mathrm{cl}}t}\delta z_0$.*

*Proof.* Substituting $u=-K_c\delta z$ into (7) and using $K_c=R^{-1}\mathcal N^\top P_c$ gives (9) by
direct algebra; the integral identity is the standard Lyapunov representation of the cost-to-go of a
Hurwitz linear system with quadratic running cost $S$. $\square$

> **Numerics.** $P_c$ (from (7)) and $P_{\text{Lyap}}$ (from (9)) agree to
> $\lVert P_{\text{Lyap}}-P_c\rVert/\lVert P_c\rVert=6\times10^{-14}$ (machine precision); the forward
> integral matches $\delta z_0^\top P_c\delta z_0$ to $\sim10^{-4}$ (finite-horizon + quadrature). These
> are checks of $P_c$ *not* using the Riccati solver.

## 5. The value-gradient identity (Doya's law) and the magnitude gate

We now make precise the property the oracle must satisfy.

**Definition 5.1 (Doya control).** For a $C^1$ reward value $V$ on the state, the *value-gradient
(Doya) control* is $u_{\mathrm D}(z):=\tfrac12R^{-1}B^\top\big(\partial_zV(z)\big)_0$, where
$(\cdot)_0=E_0(\cdot)$ extracts the current-state block (the only block $B$ acts on).

**Proposition 5.2 (exact equivalence — the gate).** *Let $V(\delta z)=-\delta z^\top P_c\,\delta z$ be
the reward value of Proposition 4.3. Then*
$$
u_{\mathrm D}(\delta z)=u^\star(\delta z)=-K_c\,\delta z\qquad\text{for all }\delta z .
$$
*In particular $\cos\angle(u_{\mathrm D},u^\star)=1$ and $|u_{\mathrm D}|/|u^\star|=1$ identically.*

*Proof.* $\partial_zV=-2P_c\delta z$, so
$u_{\mathrm D}=\tfrac12R^{-1}B^\top(-2P_c\delta z)_0=-R^{-1}B^\top(P_c\delta z)_0$. Since
$\mathcal N=E_0^\top B$, one has $\mathcal N^\top P_c\delta z=B^\top(P_c\delta z)_0$, whence
$K_c\delta z=R^{-1}\mathcal N^\top P_c\delta z=R^{-1}B^\top(P_c\delta z)_0$ and therefore
$u_{\mathrm D}=-K_c\delta z=u^\star$. The factor $\tfrac12$ cancels the factor $2$ produced by
differentiating the quadratic form; this is exactly consistent with the running cost having $u^\top Ru$
(not $\tfrac12u^\top Ru$). $\square$

We call the numerical check $|u_{\mathrm D}|/|u^\star|=1$ (and $\cos=1$) the **magnitude gate**. By
Proposition 5.2 it holds *by construction* for the continuous oracle; its role is to certify the
implementation (factor, sign, gradient block, and — crucially — that $P_c$ is a *continuous*-time
Riccati solution).

> **Remark 5.3 (the discrete-time artefact the gate guards against).** Suppose one instead used a
> *discrete* value $V_{\mathrm d}(\delta z)=-\delta z^\top P_{\mathrm d}\delta z$, where $P_{\mathrm d}$
> solves the discrete Riccati equation for the forward-Euler model
> $\delta z_{k+1}=(I+\Delta t\,M)\delta z_k+\Delta t\,\mathcal N u_k$ with cost
> $\sum_k(\delta z_k^\top\widehat Q\delta z_k+u_k^\top Ru_k)$ — note $R$ *not* scaled by $\Delta t$. The
> discrete-optimal gain is $K_{\mathrm d}=(R+\mathcal N_{\mathrm d}^\top P_{\mathrm d}\mathcal N_{\mathrm d})^{-1}\mathcal N_{\mathrm d}^\top P_{\mathrm d}A_{\mathrm d}$ with
> $\mathcal N_{\mathrm d}=\Delta t\,\mathcal N$, $A_{\mathrm d}=I+\Delta t\,M$, so
> $$
> K_{\mathrm d}=\big(R+\Delta t^2\,\mathcal N^\top P_{\mathrm d}\mathcal N\big)^{-1}\Delta t\,\mathcal N^\top P_{\mathrm d}(I+\Delta tM)
> =\Delta t\,R^{-1}\mathcal N^\top P_{\mathrm d}+O(\Delta t^2),
> $$
> whereas the Doya control built from $V_{\mathrm d}$ is $u_{\mathrm D}=-R^{-1}\mathcal N^\top P_{\mathrm d}\delta z$
> (no $\Delta t$). Hence $|u_{\mathrm D}|/|u^\star_{\mathrm{discrete}}|=\Delta t^{-1}+O(1)$: the Doya
> control overshoots by a factor $\approx1/\Delta t$. (With the platoon's solver step this is
> $\approx22.7$.) The continuous oracle eliminates the artefact at the root by never discretising time;
> the gate $|u_{\mathrm D}|/|u^\star|=1$ is precisely the certificate that this artefact is absent. $\blacksquare$

## 6. The nonlinear oracle (Pontryagin / HJB)

Now case **(N)**: $\dot z=F(z,u)$ with $F$ from (5) and $\mathcal F$ genuinely nonlinear. The linear
value of §4 is quadratic in $\delta z$; to obtain a **non-polynomial** value (the object on which the
signature-vs-monomial comparison, H2, is non-degenerate) we solve the nonlinear problem directly.

**Mackey–Glass specialisation.** $d=1$, $\mathcal F(x,y)=-\mu x+p\,g(y)$, $g(y)=y/(1+y^n)$;
$\mu=0.1,\,p=0.2,\,n=10,\,\tau=6$. The equilibrium is $x^\star=(p/\mu-1)^{1/n}=1$ with
$u^\star_{\mathrm{eq}}=0$. The linearisation coefficients are $A_0=-\mu$ and
$$
A_1=p\,g'(x^\star),\qquad g'(y)=\frac{1+(1-n)y^{\,n}}{(1+y^{\,n})^2},\qquad g'(1)=\frac{2-n}{4}=-2,
$$
so $A_1=-0.4$. Since $\tau=6>\tau_c\approx4.71$ (the Hopf threshold for these parameters), $x^\star$ is
an unstable focus surrounded by a stable limit cycle; this is why $V^\star$ is materially nonlinear.

**The minimum principle.** With cost-to-go convention and costate $\lambda\in\mathbb R^{d(N+1)}$, define
the Hamiltonian
$$
H(z,\lambda,u)=\ell(z,u)+\lambda^\top F(z,u)
=(z-z^\star)^\top\widehat Q(z-z^\star)+u^\top Ru+\lambda^\top F(z,u).
\tag{10}
$$

**Proposition 6.1 (stationary control = Doya's law).** *Since $\partial_uF=\mathcal N=E_0^\top B$, the
stationarity condition $\partial_uH=0$ gives*
$$
2Ru+\mathcal N^\top\lambda=0\ \Longrightarrow\ u^\star=-\tfrac12R^{-1}B^\top\lambda_0 .
\tag{11}
$$
*If $V^\star_{\mathrm c}$ is the (cost-to-go) value and $\lambda(t)=\partial_zV^\star_{\mathrm c}(z(t))$
along an optimal trajectory, then in reward convention $V^\star=-V^\star_{\mathrm c}$ one has
$u^\star=\tfrac12R^{-1}B^\top(\partial_zV^\star)_0=u_{\mathrm D}$: the magnitude gate holds for the
nonlinear value as well.*

*Proof.* $H$ is strictly convex in $u$ ($R\succ0$), so the stationary point (11) is the unique
minimiser, giving Pontryagin's optimal control. The identification $\lambda=\partial_zV^\star_{\mathrm c}$
along optimal trajectories is the costate/sensitivity relation of the minimum principle, equivalently
the method of characteristics for the HJB equation
$$
\min_u\big[\,\ell(z,u)+\partial_zV^\star_{\mathrm c}(z)^\top F(z,u)\,\big]=0
\tag{12}
$$
(Liberzon, *Calculus of Variations and Optimal Control Theory*, §4.2; Bryson–Ho, *Applied Optimal
Control*, §2.3). The sign flip $V^\star=-V^\star_{\mathrm c}$ converts (11) into $u_{\mathrm D}$. $\square$

**Costate dynamics and the boundary-value problem.** The adjoint equation $\dot\lambda=-\partial_zH$ is
$$
\dot\lambda=-\big(2\,\widehat Q(z-z^\star)+J(z)^\top\lambda\big),
\qquad
J(z):=\partial_zF(z,\cdot),
\tag{13}
$$
where $J(z)$ is $D\otimes I_d$ with block-row $0$ replaced by
$\big[\partial_x\mathcal F(\zeta_0,\zeta_N),\,0,\dots,0,\,\partial_y\mathcal F(\zeta_0,\zeta_N)\big]$
(for Mackey–Glass: $[-\mu,0,\dots,0,\,p\,g'(\zeta_N)]$, now evaluated at the *actual* delayed state,
not the equilibrium). Coupling (6) under $u^\star=-\tfrac12R^{-1}B^\top\lambda_0$ with (13) gives the
two-point boundary value problem on $[0,T]$:
$$
\dot z=F\big(z,-\tfrac12R^{-1}B^\top\lambda_0\big),\quad
\dot\lambda=-\big(2\widehat Q(z-z^\star)+J(z)^\top\lambda\big),
\tag{14}
$$
$$
z(0)=z_0,\qquad \lambda(T)=2P_c\big(z(T)-z^\star\big).
\tag{15}
$$

**Proposition 6.2 (consistency of the terminal condition).** *The terminal condition $\lambda(T)=2P_c(z(T)-z^\star)$
makes the finite-horizon problem (14)–(15) consistent with the infinite-horizon problem to order
$O(\lVert z(T)-z^\star\rVert^3)$: it imposes the gradient of the quadratic terminal cost
$\Psi(z)=(z-z^\star)^\top P_c(z-z^\star)$, which is the infinite-horizon optimal cost-to-go of the
linearisation (Prop. 4.3). Hence as $T\to\infty$ (so $z(T)\to z^\star$) the solution converges to the
infinite-horizon extremal.*

*Proof.* Near $x^\star$ the nonlinear value satisfies $V^\star_{\mathrm c}(z)=\Psi(z)+o(\lVert z-z^\star\rVert^2)$
because the linearisation is the leading quadratic model and $\Psi$ solves the linearised HJB; its
gradient is $2P_c(z-z^\star)$. Imposing this as $\lambda(T)$ is the standard receding-tail closure
(Bryson–Ho, §2.4). $\square$

**The value along characteristics.** For any $t\in[0,T]$, by Bellman optimality the tail of the
optimal trajectory from $z(t)$ is itself optimal, so
$$
V^\star_{\mathrm c}\big(z(t)\big)=\int_t^T\!\Big[(z(s)-z^\star)^\top\widehat Q(z(s)-z^\star)+u^\star(s)^\top Ru^\star(s)\Big]ds
\;+\;\Psi\big(z(T)\big).
\tag{16}
$$
Solving (14)–(15) from a family of initial data $\{z_0^{(k)}\}$ thus tiles the relevant region of state
space with exact samples $\big(z,\;V^\star_{\mathrm c}(z),\;\partial_zV^\star_{\mathrm c}(z)=\lambda\big)$
— the labelled dataset required to fit and test critics.

**Proposition 6.3 (costate gate, verifiable a posteriori).** *Let $z_0\mapsto V^\star_{\mathrm c}(z_0)$
be defined by (16). Then $\partial_{z_0}V^\star_{\mathrm c}(z_0)=\lambda(0)$. This is checked by central
finite differences: re-solving (14)–(15) at $z_0\pm\epsilon e_j$ and comparing
$\big(V^\star_{\mathrm c}(z_0+\epsilon e_j)-V^\star_{\mathrm c}(z_0-\epsilon e_j)\big)/2\epsilon$ to
$\lambda_j(0)$.*

*Proof.* The envelope/sensitivity theorem for (16): differentiating the optimal cost with respect to the
initial state returns the initial costate (Liberzon, §4.2; Bryson–Ho, §2.3). $\square$

> **Numerics (Mackey–Glass, $N\in\{14,18,24\}$).** `solve_bvp` converges (max residual $\sim10^{-6}$),
> warm-started by the linearised extremal $\delta z(t)=e^{A_{\mathrm{cl}}t}\delta z_0$,
> $\lambda(t)=2P_c\delta z(t)$. The costate gate (Prop. 6.3) holds to rel. err $\sim10^{-4}$. The value
> is genuinely non-quadratic: $R^2\big(V_{\mathrm{lin}}\!\to\!V^\star_{\mathrm c}\big)\approx0.4$–$0.5$
> along the optimal trajectory (where $V_{\mathrm{lin}}=\delta z^\top P_c\delta z$), versus $0.9993$ for
> the platoon — i.e. roughly half of $V^\star$ is non-polynomial.

## 7. Numerical realisation and validation protocol

For each cell the oracle is accepted only if all of the following pass; each certifies a distinct
property, and the *external* checks are the non-circular ones.

| # | check | certifies | result (MG limit-cycle) |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | $K_c$ stable in $N$; collocation spectrum vs RFDE roots (Prop. 3.3) | history resolution | rightmost eig $0.0242\pm0.323i$, $N$-invariant |
| C2 | magnitude gate $\cos=\lvert u_{\mathrm D}\rvert/\lvert u^\star\rvert=1$ (Prop. 5.2) | no time-discretisation artefact (Rem. 5.3) | $1.0000$ / $1.0000$ |
| C3 | $P_c$ via Lyapunov (9) and forward integral | $V^\star$ value, *sans* Riccati solver | $6\times10^{-14}$ / $\sim10^{-4}$ |
| C4 | costate gate $\lambda(0)=\partial_{z_0}V^\star_{\mathrm c}$ (Prop. 6.3) | nonlinear value gradient | rel. err $\sim10^{-4}$ |
| C5 | **free response** of (6) vs the true plant | collocation model $=$ plant | $\max\lvert x_{\mathrm{env}}-x_{\mathrm{model}}\rvert\approx10^{-5}$ |
| C6 | closed-loop cost on the true nonlinear plant | the oracle controls the real system | linear oracle: $98.1\%$ cost reduction |

> **Diagnostic value of C5.** Checks C2–C4 are *internal* (they relate $P_c$, $\lambda$, $V^\star$ to one
> another) and therefore pass even for a mis-specified model — they certify self-consistency, not
> fidelity. The free-response comparison C5 against the true plant is the external check; it is what
> exposed the missing $2/\tau$ factor (Caution 3.2), which had left C2–C4 *passing* on the wrong
> ($\tau=2$) model. The residual open-loop mismatch under the optimal control ($\approx0.09$ for
> Mackey–Glass) is the zero-order-hold of the *continuous* $u^\star$ at the actuation period $\Delta t$,
> not a model error (C5 itself, free of control, matches to $10^{-5}$).

## 8. Scope, hypotheses, and caveats

1. **Extremal vs. global optimum.** The Pontryagin BVP returns an *extremal* — a trajectory satisfying
   the first-order necessary conditions. For a convex-in-$u$ cost, $C^2$ mildly nonlinear dynamics, and a
   stabilisable equilibrium, the extremal continuously connected to the LQR optimum (via the warm start)
   is the optimum; global optimality is asserted on these grounds, not proved. Sufficiency would require
   a second-order/conjugate-point or HJB-verification argument.
2. **Collocation order.** All statements are for the order-$N$ collocation (6); they converge to the
   RFDE oracle as $N\to\infty$ by Proposition 3.3. The reported quantities are $N$-invariant to the
   stated digits over $N\in\{10,\dots,40\}$, except the single-trajectory nonlinearity ratio of §6,
   which drifts in $[0.38,0.54]$ and is only an indicator pending the multi-IC dataset.
3. **Hypotheses 4.2** (stabilisability/detectability) are assumed and hold in the cells considered; they
   are necessary for the CARE (7) to admit a unique stabilising $P_c$.
4. **Actuation.** The oracle control is continuous; deployed at a finite control period $\Delta t$ via
   zero-order hold it incurs an $O(\Delta t)$ open-loop error (C5 remark), corrected under feedback.
